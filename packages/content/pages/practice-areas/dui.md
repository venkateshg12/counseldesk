# DUI Defense in Arizona

Arizona has some of the toughest drunk and impaired driving laws in the country. Even a first arrest can mean jail time, large fines and losing your license for a while.

## What we handle

We defend drivers charged with any kind of DUI anywhere in Arizona. It might have started with a traffic stop, a roadside checkpoint or a crash. Either way, we check the police records, how the breath test machine was set up and kept, and how any blood or breath test was done. Mistakes there can change a case.

### First-offense DUI

This is your first DUI charge. In Arizona it usually means police say your blood alcohol was 0.08 percent or higher, or that alcohol, drugs or fumes made you unfit to drive. The usual penalties are a minimum amount of jail time, court fees, an alcohol screening or class, and an ignition interlock. That is a breathalyzer wired into your car that you must blow into before it will start.

### Extreme DUI

An Extreme DUI means your blood alcohol was 0.15 percent or higher within two hours of driving. The penalties are harsher: more minimum jail time, bigger fines and a longer time with the ignition interlock.

### Felony DUI

A felony DUI, also called an Aggravated DUI, is the most serious kind. It happens when something makes the case worse. Common examples are driving while your license is suspended or revoked, having three DUIs within seven years, or driving impaired with a child under fifteen in the car.

## How a DUI case works

A DUI case runs on two separate tracks at the same time. One is with the Motor Vehicle Division (MVD), which decides whether you can keep driving. The other is the criminal court case, which decides the penalties. The court case usually goes through your first court date, meetings before trial, sharing of evidence, hearings about whether evidence can be used, and sometimes a trial in front of a judge or jury.

## Protecting your licence

After an arrest, the officer usually takes your license and gives you a paper that says it is suspended. The same paper works as a temporary permit to drive. To keep your right to drive, you must ask for an MVD hearing quickly. We represent you at that hearing. We question why you were stopped, whether the arrest was lawful and whether the test was done properly.
