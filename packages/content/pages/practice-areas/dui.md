# DUI Defense in Arizona

Arizona enforces some of the strictest driving under the influence laws in the United States, carrying mandatory jail sentences, substantial fines, and license suspensions even for standard initial citations.

## What we handle

Our defense team represents motorists facing the entire spectrum of impaired driving charges throughout Arizona. Whether an arrest stemmed from a late-night traffic stop, an unexpected roadside sobriety checkpoint, or a multi-vehicle collision, we examine police records, calibrate breath test machines, and identify defects in chemical testing methods.

### First-offense DUI

A standard first-offense DUI in Arizona involves an alleged blood alcohol concentration of 0.08 percent or greater, or driving while impaired by alcohol, drugs, or toxic vapors. Penalties include mandatory minimum jail time, expensive court surcharges, participation in alcohol screening programs, and the installation of a certified ignition interlock device on any vehicle you operate.

### Extreme DUI

An Extreme DUI charge applies when an individual's blood alcohol concentration is determined to be 0.15 percent or higher within two hours of operating a motor vehicle. Arizona statutes prescribe significantly harsher mandatory minimum jail sentences, steeper statutory assessments, and extended periods of mandatory ignition interlock usage for convictions at this level.

### Felony DUI

Felony DUI, also termed Aggravated DUI in Arizona, arises when specific aggravating factors accompany an alleged impaired driving incident. Common aggravating circumstances include operating a vehicle with a suspended or revoked driver's license, accumulating three DUI violations within seven years, or driving while impaired with a passenger under fifteen years of age.

## How a DUI case works

A DUI proceeding in Arizona operates across two distinct tracks: the administrative process with the Motor Vehicle Division regarding driving privileges, and the criminal court case regarding statutory penalties. The criminal case advances through initial arraignment, pretrial conferences, evidence disclosure exchanges, suppression hearings, and potentially trial before a jury or judge.

## Protecting your licence

When an officer confiscates your physical driver's license following an arrest, you are issued an administrative suspension notice that also serves as a temporary driving permit. Safeguarding your driving privileges requires requesting an administrative hearing promptly. Our attorneys represent clients during these Motor Vehicle Division hearings, challenging the officer's initial reasonable suspicion, detention legality, and chemical testing compliance.
