# Arizona Divorce Frequently Asked Questions

Dissolving a marriage involves legal procedures governed by Arizona domestic relations statutes and local county court rules.

## Filing

To initiate a divorce in Arizona, either spouse must file a Petition for Dissolution of Marriage in the Superior Court of the county where they reside. At least one spouse must have maintained continuous domicile within Arizona for a minimum of 90 days prior to filing. Once filed, the petition and accompanying summons must be formally served upon the non-filing spouse.

## The waiting period

Divorce in Arizona has a 60-day waiting period after service. Under state law, courts cannot enter a final decree of dissolution until this statutory cooling-off period elapses, even if both spouses have reached complete agreement on all issues. This timeframe allows parties to reflect, consult counsel, and negotiate comprehensive settlement agreements.

## Property and debts

Arizona is a community property state, creating a legal presumption that all assets and debts acquired by either spouse during the marriage belong equally to both parties. Separate property, such as assets acquired prior to marriage or through personal inheritance, remains individual property unless commingled. Courts divide community assets and obligations equitably upon final dissolution.
